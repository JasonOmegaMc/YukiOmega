# Kuzunoha, the fox-wife

**Chapter:** "The Fox Who Left Her Shadow" (working title)

**Claim in the retelling:** The legend of Kuzunoha: a fox who lives as a
wife and mother, revealed when her true nature shows in the child's
shadow or her own reflection, and who leaves a farewell poem for her son.

**Sources:**

- The Edo-period jōruri/kabuki play *Ashiya Dōman Ōuchi Kagami*
  (1734) — the full dramatic frame: Abe no Yasuna, the white fox saved
  in the Sumiyoshi reeds, the child who grows into the diviner Abe no
  Seimei.
- The farewell poem (the "chin-enoki" / neko-no-eda tradition): the
  child's verse returns as the mother's confession, a tanka on seeing
  and being seen. Versions collected in Meiji-era folkloristics and
  later in Yanagita's circle's compilations.
- The historical anchor: Abe no Seimei (d. 1005) was a real onmyōji
  of the Heian court; his mother's obscurity left a door the fox legend
  walked through.

**Where it falls short:** The 1734 play is Edo theatre, not Heian
record. The legend's older strata are fragmented; the shadow motif
appears in some tellings, the mirror in others.

**Marked embellishment in the chapter:** all dialogue; the child's age;
the season of the leaving.
