# Vanlandi and the mara

**Chapter:** "The King Who Was Ridden"
**Claim in the retelling:** Ynglinga saga records that King Vanlandi of
Sweden was killed by a mara (nightmare spirit) summoned by the sorceress
Hulda, after he broke his promise to return to Drífa, daughter of the
Sámi king Snjá the Old, within three years.

**Primary source:** Snorri Sturluson, *Ynglinga saga*, ch. 16. Old Norse
text in standard editions (e.g. Heimskringla, ed. Finnur Jónsson); the
account also appears in verse form in *Ynglingatal*, attributed to
Þjóðólfr of Hvin (9th c.), which is the older layer Snorri quotes.

**Where it falls short:** The saga is 13th-century prose arranging
9th-century skaldic verse about legendary kings. Vanlandi is not firmly
attested history; what is "true bones" here is that the mara as a
sleep-paralysis figure is the genuine old Scandinavian belief the story
preserves, and that the Sámi-sorcery frame is Snorri's era's shorthand
for the north.

**Marked embellishment in the chapter:** the sensory details of the
first two nights, the firelight; the shape of Drífa's waiting is my
inference from the saga's laconic "he did not come."
