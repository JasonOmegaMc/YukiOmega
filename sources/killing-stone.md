# Tamamo-no-Mae and the Killing Stone

**Chapter:** "The Stone That Kills"

**Claim in the retelling:** The courtesan Tamamo-no-Mae, favored by
Emperor Toba, was unmasked as a nine-tailed fox and fled to the plain of
Nasu, where she became (or haunted) the stone Sessho-seki — the stone
that kills what touches it — until the monk Gennō broke its curse.

**Sources:**

- *Tamamo no Sōshi* (otogi-zōshi, Muromachi period, 15th c.) — the
  full arc: fox as court lady, divination unmasking, flight, the stone.
- The stone itself: Sessho-seki in Nasu, Tochigi Prefecture, real and
  visitable; the surrounding area long avoided by locals, sulfur vents
  and animal bones at the site — plausibly the seed of the legend.
- Gennō Shōkai (14th c.) and the "breaking" episode; the stone's
  reported splitting in 1957 is local news history, not legend.

**Where it falls short:** Tamamo-no-Mae's connection to Emperor
Toba's court is legend layered on a real reign (1143–55). The
nine-tails identity is a later graft onto earlier fox-wife strata,
possibly tying Tamamo to the fox-of-Dōji thread.

**Marked embellishment in the chapter:** the fox's interiority
throughout; any scene at the stone that reads like reportage is the
retelling's invention dressed in the site's documented features.
